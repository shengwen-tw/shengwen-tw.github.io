## Overview

From September 19 to 28, I traveled to California. After working at NVIDIA Taiwan for two and a half years, I wanted to see the headquarters for myself, and also to get a feel for the engineering and startup culture in the Bay Area. During the trip, I met several friends who work or run startups in Silicon Valley, and talking with them taught me a lot about the place. Here I write down the parts of the trip that meant the most to me.

## NVIDIA

The first major stop of the trip was NVIDIA's headquarters in Santa Clara. NVIDIA has several buildings there, and the two most iconic are Endeavor (opened in 2017) and Voyager (opened in 2022). Both are designed around natural light, greenery, and openness, with glass everywhere. I also had the opportunity to meet several colleagues at the US headquarters whom I work with regularly. Over lunch, we chatted about how they spend their days off, from hiking to car racing.

## Nuro

On the second day, I visited [Nuro](https://www.nuro.ai/) to meet Tony Chen, a friend I first got to know online who recently graduated from the University of Washington. Seattle was the first place I ever visited in the United States, so hearing about his time there brought back many memories, including the beautiful UW campus.

Tony is friendly and ambitious. He shared a lot about what is happening in the Bay Area, especially the startup culture here. Nuro was founded by former Waymo engineers, so it was a great chance to hear first-hand from someone working directly in the autonomous driving industry.

## Waymo

Something unique about California is seeing [Waymo](https://waymo.com/) robotaxis driving everywhere, and it was one of the things that fascinated me most here. The ride was smooth overall, but I could feel a small bump every time it braked to a stop. Nevertheless, the future is already here: the most innovative self-driving companies, from Waymo and Nuro/Lucid to Tesla's Cybercab, are all based in the United States, and their technology is already on the road.

## IrisGo.AI

I also met [Lman Chu](https://lmanchu.github.io/), an entrepreneur and now co-founder and COO of [IrisGo.AI](https://www.irisgo.ai/). I first got to know Lman almost ten years ago, when I had the chance to demonstrate some of my university projects at an event he organized, and I later provided some lightweight support for one of his businesses at [Computex 2024](https://www.adlinktech.com/en/news/simprobot-tallgeeseai-2024).

IrisGo.AI builds an AI assistant for AI PCs that learns a user's workflows and automates repetitive tasks across applications. The company was co-founded with [AI Fund](https://aifund.ai/portfolio/irisgo/), founded by Andrew Ng, where Lman joined as a founder in residence. AI Fund also led their seed round. It was a unique experience to see first-hand how an early-stage startup operates here in Silicon Valley.

## Computer History Museum

One of the things that shaped my career the most was reading [*Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackers:_Heroes_of_the_Computer_Revolution) by Steven Levy in high school. It was the book that gave me my first impression of computer technology and its free spirit, and Silicon Valley has been a place I wanted to visit ever since. The [Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/) is something like a spiritual temple to me. Before this trip, I never thought I would get the chance to see historical artifacts like the DEC PDP-11 (the machine that early UNIX ran on), ENIAC, and Enigma in person.

## Stanford University

Besides the tech companies, I also visited Stanford University, located in the heart of Silicon Valley. I checked the course schedule for the day of our visit and found that Andrew Ng would be teaching [Deep Learning (CS230)](https://cs230.stanford.edu/) that day. Since high school, I have always enjoyed studying online courses, especially those offered by top universities like Stanford and MIT. Getting to sit in the classroom of one of these courses in person made this a special experience for me.
